What the tester found, and what we did

Two rounds of testing, twenty-six findings between them. All fixed.

  • The reply box vanished on a laptop. At common laptop and iPad widths the box you type into had no room left at all — just a send arrow and nowhere to write. Our fix last week stopped the page scrolling sideways but pushed the squeeze inside the cards, which was worse. Properly fixed now, and checked at every width from 901 to 1440.
  • Columns were being hidden with no way to reach them. On All Leads the Stage and Next Action columns simply disappeared on a narrower screen. They scroll now, so nothing is lost.
  • The dashboard was cutting off stages and telling you to scroll when there was nothing to scroll to. It measures itself now: the hint only appears when something really is out of view.
  • The Integrations panel said Aircall was not connected, while it was. That panel was a box someone had to tick by hand, so it could say anything. It checks the real state now and explains what it found. The same box could have claimed a channel was live when it was carrying nothing, which is the worse way round.
  • Dates came in three different styles on one screen. Everything now reads like "1 Sep 2026". The two deadline counters also counted different kinds of day without saying so; the one that skips weekends now says "working days".
  • Buttons too small to hit on a phone. Several were the right height but too narrow. All of them clear the 44-pixel minimum in both directions now.
  • Test data was indistinguishable from real. Every invented lead carries a TEST label, wherever it appears. This is the one that matters most from here: when your first real enquiry lands, you will know it is real without having to recognise a name.

Also: times on an open page stopped updating — a dashboard left alone said "19h" for half an hour after it should have said "20h", and now refreshes itself. A failed page load used to show zeros everywhere, which read as a business with no customers rather than a page that had not loaded; it says so plainly now. Plus the keyboard focus ring on the dashboard, a button in the conversation header that did nothing, and a counter that said 11 and opened a page showing 6.

One we caught before it bit

Clearing out the old demo data left the Lead Centre empty, which was the point — so your first real enquiry arrives into a clean inbox. But the app rebuilds its starter data whenever it finds an empty database, and the next routine update would have poured nineteen invented customers straight back in, alongside anything real that had arrived.

Found and fixed the same day. Worth telling you because it is the kind of thing that would have been very confusing a week later.

Still outstanding

Six of the twenty-six. None of them stops the Lead Centre working, and we would rather you saw the list than took our word that everything was done.

  • A conversation on a small phone screen still feels cramped. We got the message area from about 250 pixels to just over 300, which is better but still under half the screen. Getting properly more room means moving the bottom menu or the channel buttons out of the way inside a conversation — a design decision rather than a fix, and we would rather agree it with you than guess.
  • The unread badge is per browser, not per person. If you read your notifications on your laptop, your phone will still show them as unread. Doing it properly means the app remembering what each person has read, which is a change to how it stores things. Worth doing, not urgent.
  • The lost lead is a number, not something you can click. The dashboard says "1 lost" as plain text. Every other figure there opens the matching list; this one does not yet.
  • Settings forgets which tab you were on. Refresh the page while looking at Integrations and it goes back to General.
  • Settings → General does not say who can change what. It shows your account, the team and the 30-minute target, all read-only, without saying who to ask. The labels themselves are clear.
  • The notifications panel does not hold keyboard focus. Opening it with a keyboard and pressing Tab walks out of the panel instead of moving through it. Escape does close it.

Say the word on any of these and we will pick them up. The first two are the ones we would do next.

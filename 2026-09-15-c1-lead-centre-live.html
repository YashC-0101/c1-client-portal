Two accounts, one for each of you. Best on a phone — that's how the crew would use it.

Unlocking the sign-in details…

These two are demo accounts. They share one password and everything in them is made up — no real customer, no real job. When you send us the real staff list we replace them with proper accounts, each with its own password, before a single real enquiry lands.

What's done

  • The app itself — the unified inbox, the lead record with photos, postcode, access notes and volume, the pipeline stages, and the reply flow.
  • Demo data — enough leads, numbers and messages to use it as if it were a working week.
  • Deployed — up and reachable, though still on a temporary web address. It moves to a proper one before go-live.
  • CI/CD — every change is tested and deployed automatically, so fixes reach you the same day rather than in a batch.

What's still to build

Being straight about what isn't finished. The first three matter most.

  • The WhatsApp connection. The app only speaks Aircall today, so a different provider means building a second connection. Our cost, not yours.
  • Photos have nowhere to go. You quote off photos, and right now anything sent over WhatsApp arrives and gets dropped.
  • Nobody gets told when it breaks. No alerting yet — if the connection fails, enquiries stop and the inbox just looks quiet. Worst way for a lead system to fail.
  • Call recordings get binned, backups aren't confirmed yet, and the Numbers & Routing screen still shows the old porting plan that's now cancelled.
  • Smaller stuff: no password reset, everyone can see every screen, the lead list loads all at once, and it's still on a temporary web address.

Why nothing real can arrive yet

The inbox is wired to Aircall, which carries voice, SMS and WhatsApp on one number. Until the Aircall credentials are set, the webhook refuses every incoming event, and a reply is marked recorded locally — not sent rather than pretending it went out. That's deliberate: an inbox that half works and quietly drops an enquiry would be worse than one that plainly says it's off.
